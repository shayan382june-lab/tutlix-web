⚡ D2.1 in 60 seconds

  1. New cells come from division of existing cells; cytokinesis splits the cytoplasm.
  2. Mitosis → identical nuclei; meiosis → haploid, varied nuclei.
  3. Phases: P-M-A-T; chromosomes condense first.
  4. Variation from crossing over and random orientation.
  5. Non-disjunction → trisomy 21 (Down syndrome).
  6. HL: cell cycle, cyclins, cancer, mitotic index.

D2.1.1 – D2.1.4

Cell division and cytokinesis

  • New cells are only produced by division of pre-existing cells.
  • Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm into two cells after nuclear division.
  • It is usually equal, but can be unequal: e.g. oogenesis (one large egg + small polar bodies) and budding in yeast.
  • Both daughter cells must receive at least one nucleus and some mitochondria (and chloroplasts in plants).
