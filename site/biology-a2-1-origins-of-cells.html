⚡ A2.1 in 60 seconds

  1. Early Earth had no free oxygen, strong UV, lightning and volcanoes: conditions where simple carbon compounds could form.
  2. A cell is the smallest unit of self-sustaining life; viruses are not cells.
  3. Making a cell needs catalysis, self-replication, self-assembly and compartmentalisation.
  4. Miller–Urey made amino acids from simple gases + sparks; fatty acids form vesicles on their own.
  5. RNA can store information and catalyse reactions, so it was probably the first genetic material.
  6. All life descends from LUCA, which probably lived at hydrothermal vents around 4 billion years ago.
A2.1.1

Conditions on early Earth

Earth formed about 4.5 billion years ago. Conditions then were very different from today, and allowed simple carbon compounds to form without living things (pre-biotic chemistry).

  • Atmosphere: likely methane, ammonia, hydrogen, water vapour and carbon dioxide, with little or no free oxygen. Without oxygen, newly formed carbon compounds were not broken down by oxidation.
  • No ozone layer, so strong UV radiation reached the surface, providing energy for reactions.
  • Lightning, volcanoes and high temperatures provided more energy.

"My Aunt Has Water"

The gases Miller used: Methane, Ammonia, Hydrogen, Water vapour. And remember: no O₂.
