⚡ A3.1 in 60 seconds

  1. Variation is a defining feature of life, but organisms group into species.
  2. Binomial names: Genus species, in italics.
  3. Biological species: can interbreed and produce fertile offspring; boundaries blur during speciation.
  4. Chromosome numbers vary; karyograms pair chromosomes by size, centromere and bands.
  5. Genomes are nearly identical within a species (SNPs differ) but vary hugely in size between species.
  6. HL: species concept problems (asexual, bacteria) · infertile hybrids · dichotomous keys · eDNA.

Variation and species

No two organisms are exactly alike: variation is a defining feature of life. Despite this, organisms can be grouped into species: groups that share many traits.

  • Early naturalists such as Linnaeus grouped organisms by visible features. This is the morphological species concept.
  • Problem: individuals of one species can look very different (e.g. dog breeds), and different species can look very similar.
