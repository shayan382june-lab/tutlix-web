⚡ B4.1 in 60 seconds

  1. A habitat is where an organism normally lives.
  2. Adaptations suit abiotic conditions: marram grass (dunes), mangroves (salty mud).
  3. Abiotic factors set distribution; each species has a range of tolerance.
  4. Reefs need shallow, warm, salty, alkaline, clear water.
  5. Temperature and rainfall decide biomes; similar conditions → convergent evolution.
  6. Desert plants save water; rainforest plants shed water and race for light.

B4.1.1

Habitats

A habitat is the place where a community, species, population or organism normally lives. A good description includes its geographical location and the type of ecosystem, e.g. "the rocky shore of the Atlantic coast of Ireland" or "the canopy of tropical rainforest in Borneo".
