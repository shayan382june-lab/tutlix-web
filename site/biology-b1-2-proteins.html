⚡ B1.2 in 60 seconds

  1. Amino acids: central C with amine, carboxyl, H and R group.
  2. They join by condensation into peptide bonds.
  3. Some are essential and must come from food; variety is 20n.
  4. Heat and pH denature proteins by changing their 3D shape.
  5. HL: R groups vary; structure has 4 levels; tertiary held by H, ionic, disulfide and hydrophobic interactions.
  6. HL: globular (insulin) vs fibrous (collagen); conjugated (haemoglobin) vs non-conjugated.

Amino acids and peptide bonds

Proteins are polymers of amino acids. All amino acids share the same basic structure:

Amino acids join by condensation: the carboxyl group of one reacts with the amine group of the next, forming a peptide bond and releasing water. Two amino acids make a dipeptide; many make a polypeptide.

🧠 Memory trick

"Amine, Carboxyl, H, R": A Cat Has Rabies

The four groups around the central carbon. Only R changes between amino acids.
