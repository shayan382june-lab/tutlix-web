⚡ B3.3 in 60 seconds

  1. Organisms are motile or sessile, but all move in some way.
  2. Sliding filaments: myosin heads pull actin; sarcomeres shorten; ATP and Ca²⁺ needed.
  3. Titin and antagonistic muscles return muscles to resting length.
  4. A motor unit = motor neuron + its fibres; more units = more force.
  5. Skeletons anchor muscles and act as levers; the hip is a synovial ball-and-socket joint.
  6. Locomotion for food, safety, mates and migration; marine mammals are streamlined with flippers and blowholes.

B3.3.1 · HL

Movement: a feature of life

All living organisms move in some way. Some are motile: they can move from place to place (e.g. animals, many bacteria). Others are sessile: fixed in one place (e.g. most plants, corals, barnacles), but they still move parts of themselves (e.g. leaves turning to light, barnacles' feeding legs).
