⚡ C1.2 in 60 seconds

  1. ATP distributes energy: soluble, stable, quickly regenerated.
  2. Respiration releases energy from organic compounds to make ATP.
  3. Aerobic: needs O₂, lots of ATP, CO₂ + water. Anaerobic (humans): lactate, 2 ATP.
  4. HL: NAD carries H; glycolysis → 2 pyruvate, 2 ATP, 2 NADH.
  5. HL: link reaction and Krebs cycle: decarboxylation and oxidation.
  6. HL: ETC pumps H⁺; chemiosmosis through ATP synthase; O₂ is the final electron acceptor.

ATP: the energy currency

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is a nucleotide: adenine + ribose + three phosphate groups. It is the molecule that distributes energy within cells because it is:

  • Soluble, so it moves easily around the cell
  • Stable at cell pH, so it doesn't break down until an enzyme acts
  • Releases a convenient amount of energy when hydrolysed: enough for most cell processes, not wasteful
  • Can be quickly regenerated: ADP + Pi → ATP, using energy from respiration (each ATP is recycled thousands of times a day)

ATP supplies energy for active transport, movement (e.g. muscle contraction, moving vesicles and chromosomes) and anabolic reactions (synthesising macromolecules such as DNA and proteins).

🧠 Memory trick

ATP is like a rechargeable battery

Respiration charges ADP to ATP; cell processes spend it, turning ATP back to ADP + Pi.
