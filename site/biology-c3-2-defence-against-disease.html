⚡ C3.2 in 60 seconds

  1. Pathogens cause infectious diseases.
  2. Skin and mucous membranes are the first barrier; clotting seals cuts.
  3. Innate (phagocytes, non-specific) vs adaptive (lymphocytes, specific, memory).
  4. B cells activated by helper T cells clone into plasma cells (antibodies) and memory cells.
  5. HIV destroys helper T cells → AIDS; antibiotics kill bacteria not viruses; resistance evolves.
  6. Vaccines give memory without disease; herd immunity protects populations.

Pathogens and the first lines of defence

Pathogens are organisms or viruses that cause infectious disease: viruses, bacteria, fungi and protists (and parasitic animals).

  • Skin: a tough outer layer of dead keratinised cells is a physical barrier; sebaceous glands secrete oils and lactic acid that keep the surface acidic, slowing microbial growth.
  • Mucous membranes (airways, gut, urogenital tract) secrete sticky mucus that traps pathogens; cilia sweep it away; mucus contains the enzyme lysozyme.
