D4.1.1 – D4.1.6

How natural selection works

  • Natural selection is the main mechanism of evolution: the change in heritable characteristics of a population over generations.
  • Variation comes from mutation (new alleles) and sexual reproduction (meiosis + random fertilisation make new combinations).
  • Organisms overproduce offspring, so there is competition for limited resources.
  • Selection pressures, including abiotic factors (temperature, water, pH), predators and disease, mean some individuals are better adapted.
  • Better-adapted individuals survive and reproduce more, passing on their alleles.
  • Only heritable traits are selected. Characteristics acquired during a lifetime (e.g. bigger muscles from training) are not inherited.
🧠 Memory trick

"Very Old Cats Still Reproduce Happily"

Variation → Overproduction → Competition → Survival of the best adapted → Reproduction → Heritable alleles become more common.
