⚡ B1.1 in 60 seconds

  1. Carbon forms 4 covalent bonds: chains, rings, huge variety.
  2. Polymers form by condensation and break down by hydrolysis.
  3. Glucose: soluble, stable, energy-rich; α and β differ at carbon 1.
  4. Starch/glycogen store energy (α-glucose, branched); cellulose gives strength (β-glucose, microfibrils).
  5. Glycoproteins allow cell recognition (ABO blood groups).
  6. Lipids: triglycerides store energy and insulate; phospholipids form bilayers; steroids cross membranes.

Why carbon?

Life is based on carbon compounds. Each carbon atom can form four covalent bonds, allowing an almost endless variety of molecules:

  • Chains of any length, straight or branched
  • Rings, like glucose
  • Single and double bonds
  • Bonds to other elements: H, O, N, P, S
💡 Exam tip

Covalent bonds are strong and stable, so large carbon molecules (macromolecules) hold their shape.
