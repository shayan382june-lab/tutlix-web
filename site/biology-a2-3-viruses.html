⚡ A2.3 in 60 seconds

  1. Viruses are non-cellular: nucleic acid in a protein capsid, no cytoplasm, few enzymes.
  2. They vary: DNA or RNA, single or double strand, with or without an envelope (λ phage, coronavirus, HIV).
  3. Lytic cycle: attach, inject, synthesise, assemble, lyse.
  4. Lysogenic cycle: viral DNA integrates as a prophage and is copied with the host.
  5. Viruses probably have several origins; similarities are convergent evolution.
  6. Viruses evolve fast: new flu vaccines every year; HIV resists drugs and vaccines.

A2.3.1 · HL

What all viruses have in common

Viruses are non-cellular infectious particles. They can only reproduce inside a host cell, so they are obligate intracellular parasites.

  • Very small, usually 20–300 nm, and a fixed size for each type (they don't grow).
  • Genetic material is DNA or RNA, never both.
  • A capsid: a protein coat made of repeating subunits.
  • No cytoplasm, and few or no enzymes of their own, so they use the host's ribosomes and enzymes.
🧠 Memory trick

"Small Nasty Capsules, No Cytoplasm"

Small, fixed size · Nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) · Capsid of protein · No Cytoplasm, few enzymes.

⚠️ Common mistake

Don't call viruses cells, and don't say they are "killed" by antibiotics. Antibiotics target bacterial structures that viruses don't have.
