⚡ B2.1 in 60 seconds

  1. Membranes are phospholipid bilayers: barriers to large and charged particles.
  2. Simple diffusion and osmosis need no energy; facilitated diffusion uses channels.
  3. Active transport uses ATP and pumps to move particles against the gradient.
  4. Fluid mosaic model: integral and peripheral proteins, glycoproteins and glycolipids.
  5. HL: unsaturated tails and cholesterol control fluidity; vesicles allow endo/exocytosis.
  6. HL: gated channels · Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3 out, 2 in) · SGLT cotransport · CAMs.

B2.1.1 · B2.1.2

The lipid bilayer

All cell membranes are based on a phospholipid bilayer (see B1.1). Its hydrophobic core makes it a barrier:

  • Low permeability to large molecules (e.g. glucose, proteins) and to hydrophilic particles such as ions, which can't easily pass the hydrophobic core.
  • Small, non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) and some small polar ones pass easily.
