⚡ A1.1 in 60 seconds

  1. Water is polar: O is δ−, each H is δ+.
  2. Polarity causes hydrogen bonds between water molecules: weak alone, strong in huge numbers.
  3. H-bonds give cohesion (xylem transport, surface tension) and adhesion (capillary action).
  4. Water dissolves polar and charged substances, so it is the medium for metabolism and transport.
  5. Compared with air, water has more buoyancy, viscosity, thermal conductivity and specific heat capacity.
  6. HL: Earth's water probably came from asteroids; liquid water is the first thing we look for in the search for life.
A1.1.1

Water as the medium for life

The first cells most likely arose in water, and water is still the medium in which all life's chemistry happens. Cytoplasm is mostly water, and most metabolic reactions take place between molecules dissolved in it.

  • Dissolved molecules can move and collide, so they can react.
  • Water is liquid across the range of temperatures found on most of Earth's surface.
  • Water itself takes part in reactions, e.g. hydrolysis (water used to split molecules) and condensation (water released when molecules join).
💡 Exam tip

"Medium" means the substance in which something happens. If asked why water is the medium for life, link it to dissolving reactants so they can collide.

A1.1.2

Polarity and hydrogen bonds

A water molecule (H2O) has two covalent bonds, each between the oxygen atom and a hydrogen atom. Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen, so it pulls the shared electrons closer to itself. The sharing is unequal, so these are polar covalent bonds.

  • The oxygen end gets a slight negative charge (δ−).
  • Each hydrogen end gets a slight positive charge (δ+).
  • The molecule is bent (about 104.5°), so the charges don't cancel out. The whole molecule is polar.

Because water molecules are polar, the δ+ hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the δ− oxygen of a neighbouring molecule. This attraction is a hydrogen bond.

  • One hydrogen bond is weak, much weaker than a covalent bond.
  • But there are huge numbers of them, so together they are strong. This gives water its unusual properties: cohesion, adhesion, a high specific heat capacity and a high boiling point for such a small molecule.
🧠 Memory trick

"H hugs the O next door."

A hydrogen bond is always a δ+ H attracted to the δ− O of a neighbouring molecule.

⚠️ Common mistake

Hydrogen bonds are between water molecules, not within one. The bonds holding H to O inside a molecule are covalent.

A1.1.3

Cohesion

📘 Definition

Cohesion is the attraction between molecules of the same substance. In water, it is caused by hydrogen bonds between water molecules.

1. Transport in xylem

Water evaporates from leaves (transpiration), which pulls water up the xylem from the roots. The water column is under tension (negative pressure), like being sucked up a straw. Cohesion keeps the column from breaking, so it can be pulled up as one continuous stream, even to the top of tall trees.

2. Surface tension as a habitat

At the surface, water molecules are pulled strongly by the molecules beside and below them. This creates surface tension, which behaves like a thin elastic skin. Small, light organisms such as pond skaters (water striders) can stand and move on the surface without sinking.

A1.1.4

Adhesion

📘 Definition

Adhesion is the attraction between water and a different substance that is polar or charged.

  • Capillary action: adhesion pulls water along narrow spaces, against gravity. Cohesion then drags more water along behind it.
  • In plant cell walls: cellulose is polar, so water is drawn into and along cell walls. This keeps leaf cells moist and replaces water lost by evaporation.
  • In soil: water moves through tiny gaps between soil particles by capillary action, including upwards from wetter, deeper soil towards roots.
  • In xylem: adhesion to the vessel walls helps the water column resist gravity (Fig. 3).
🧠 Memory trick

COhesion = COpy · ADhesion = ADd

Cohesion: water sticks to a copy of itself. Adhesion: water sticks to something you add (glass, cellulose, soil).

A1.1.5

Water as a solvent

Because water is polar, it forms attractions with other polar molecules and with ions. Water molecules surround each particle and separate it from the others, so the substance dissolves.

💧 Hydrophilic ("water-loving")

Polar or charged. Dissolve in water or are attracted to it.

e.g. glucose, amino acids, Na+, Cl−

🛢️ Hydrophobic ("water-fearing")

Non-polar. Do not dissolve in water; they are not attracted to water molecules.

e.g. fats, oils, cholesterol

🧠 Memory trick

"Like dissolves like."

Polar water dissolves polar and charged things. Non-polar fats only mix with other non-polar substances.

A medium for metabolism

Enzymes and their substrates are dissolved in the water of the cytoplasm, so they can move, meet and react. Most of the cell's metabolism happens in solution.

A1.1.6

Water vs air for aquatic animals

Animals that live in water face very different physical conditions from animals that live in air. You need to compare four properties. Tap each card to see what it means for aquatic animals.

🧠 Memory trick

"Big Vets Treat Seals"

Buoyancy · Viscosity · Thermal conductivity · Specific heat capacity. And the ringed seal is one of your named examples.

Factors are approximate, at around 20 °C. The syllabus asks you to compare, not to memorise values.

Named examples

🐦 Black-throated loon

Gavia arctica

A diving bird. It has a streamlined body and legs set far back for swimming underwater. Its bones are denser than most birds', which reduces buoyancy so it can dive. Dense waterproof feathers reduce heat loss.

🦭 Ringed seal

Pusa hispida

A mammal of Arctic seas. A thick layer of blubber insulates against heat loss in cold water and adds buoyancy. Its streamlined shape reduces drag.

A1.1.7 · HL

Where Earth's water came from

The early Earth was very hot, so any water present at first was probably lost. The main hypothesis is that much of Earth's water was delivered later by asteroids (and possibly comets) that collided with the planet. Some asteroids contain a lot of water locked in their minerals.

Why Earth kept its water

  • Gravity: Earth is massive enough to hold on to its atmosphere, so water vapour is not lost to space.
  • Temperature: Earth's distance from the Sun keeps most surface water liquid, neither boiled away nor all frozen.
🧠 Memory trick

"Gravity Grips, Temperature Tames."

Gravity grips the atmosphere; the right temperature keeps water liquid.

A1.1.8 · HL

Water and the search for life

All known life depends on liquid water, so scientists searching for life elsewhere look first for places where liquid water could exist.

📘 Key term

The Goldilocks zone (habitable zone) is the range of distances from a star where a planet's surface temperature allows water to be liquid: not too hot, not too cold.

  • Planets outside our Solar System (exoplanets) in their star's Goldilocks zone are priority targets.
  • Moons with liquid water oceans under their ice (e.g. Europa, Enceladus) are also of interest.
  • Finding liquid water does not prove life exists. It shows that one key condition is met.

1Explain why water molecules are polar.[3]
Marking points
  • Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen.
  • Electrons in the O–H covalent bonds are shared unequally, pulled towards oxygen.
  • Oxygen becomes δ− and hydrogens δ+; the bent shape means the charges don't cancel.
2Distinguish between cohesion and adhesion, with one example of each in plants.[4]
Marking points
  • Cohesion: attraction between water molecules (hydrogen bonds).
  • Example: keeps the water column in xylem unbroken under tension.
  • Adhesion: attraction between water and other polar or charged substances.
  • Example: water drawn into cellulose cell walls / capillary action / attraction to xylem walls.
3Glucose is carried dissolved in blood plasma, but cholesterol is not. Explain why.[3]
Marking points
  • Glucose is polar / hydrophilic, so it forms hydrogen bonds with water and dissolves.
  • Cholesterol is non-polar / hydrophobic, so it is insoluble in water.
  • Cholesterol is carried inside lipoproteins.
4Suggest why a ringed seal needs more insulation than a land mammal of the same size at the same temperature.[2]
Marking points
  • Water has a much higher thermal conductivity than air.
  • So heat is lost from the body much faster in water; blubber reduces this heat loss.
5HL: Outline two reasons why liquid water has been retained on Earth.[2]
Marking points
  • Earth's gravity is strong enough to retain its atmosphere / water vapour.
  • Its distance from the Sun gives temperatures at which water stays liquid.
