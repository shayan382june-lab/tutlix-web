⚡ A3.2 in 60 seconds

  1. Classification organises and names species and lets us predict features.
  2. Traditional ranks (D K P C O F G S) don't always match evolution.
  3. A clade = a common ancestor + all its descendants.
  4. Sequence differences build up steadily: the molecular clock.
  5. Cladograms are built from base / amino acid sequences using parsimony.
  6. rRNA sequences split life into Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.

Why classify, and the problems

With millions of species, biologists need a system to organise them, name them consistently and predict features of newly discovered species.

Problems with the traditional hierarchy

  • Ranks are arbitrary: there's no rule for how different two orders must be.
  • Evolution is continuous, so groups don't always fit neatly into fixed levels.
  • Groups based on appearance may not reflect evolutionary relationships (convergent evolution can make unrelated organisms look alike).
✅ Why classify by evolution?

If groups contain organisms with a common ancestor, members are likely to share features. So we can predict characteristics of a newly found species from its relatives, e.g. which plants might contain a useful medicinal compound.
