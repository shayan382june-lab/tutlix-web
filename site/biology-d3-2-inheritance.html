D3.2.1 – D3.2.5

Genes, alleles and crosses

  • Haploid gametes (n) fuse to form a diploid zygote (2n), with two alleles of each gene.
  • Genotype: the alleles an organism has (e.g. Tt). Phenotype: its observable characteristics.
  • Homozygous = two identical alleles; heterozygous = two different alleles.
  • Dominant alleles mask recessive ones in heterozygotes. Codominant alleles are both expressed (e.g. roan cattle, AB blood group). In incomplete dominance the heterozygote is intermediate.
  • Many genetic diseases are caused by recessive alleles (e.g. cystic fibrosis); a few by dominant ones (e.g. Huntington's disease).
  • Punnett grids predict genotype and phenotype ratios: 3 : 1 for Aa × Aa; 1 : 1 for Aa × aa.
