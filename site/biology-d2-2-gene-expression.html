⚡ D2.2 in 60 seconds

  1. Gene expression is regulated at transcription (transcription factors, promoters, enhancers) and translation (mRNA degradation).
  2. Epigenesis: differentiation by switching genes on/off.
  3. Genome same in every cell; transcriptome and proteome differ.
  4. Methylation silences genes; acetylation activates them.
  5. Tags are inherited by daughter cells; most are reset in gametes.
  6. Environment matters: twin studies, pollution, hormones, lac operon.

Controlling gene expression

  • Gene expression is how the information in a gene affects the phenotype: transcription, then translation, then the protein acts.
  • Transcription is regulated by proteins that bind to specific DNA base sequences: promoters (where RNA polymerase binds), enhancers (bind activator transcription factors) and silencers (bind repressors).
  • Translation is also regulated, e.g. by controlling how quickly mRNA is degraded: a short-lived mRNA makes less protein.
